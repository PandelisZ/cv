# Isomorphic Labs ML Ops Cover Letter

Dear Isomorphic Labs team,

I am applying for the Senior Software Engineer, ML Ops role because it sits at the exact point where I have spent the last few years doing my best work: making frontier AI systems reliable enough to run real workloads for real users.

At Cosine, as CTO, I built and led large parts of the platform behind an AI coding lab. That meant far more than wrapping models in product UI. I worked across the runtime, orchestration layer, secure execution environment, observability, deployment model, and developer experience needed to turn model capability into a system people could trust. We built agents that could operate on real codebases, run tools, search repositories, use browsers, maintain state, replay runs, and execute safely in remote sandboxed environments.

The infrastructure work maps closely to the ML Ops problems in this role. I built Kubernetes-scheduled remote workers, gVisor/runsc isolation, ephemeral execution sessions, WebSocket control planes, checkpointing, replay, event logs, and deployment paths for cloud, private VPC, on-prem, and air-gapped customers. The constant tradeoff was speed versus reliability: moving quickly with model and product iteration while hardening the underlying platform enough for sensitive enterprise environments.

What attracts me to Isomorphic Labs is that the infrastructure has unusually high leverage. Your models are not abstract demos; they sit inside a drug design engine that scientists use to reason about biology, molecules, and disease. That makes reliability, throughput, operational clarity, and platform maturity matter in a very concrete way. If the inference platform, workload orchestration, and model-serving stack work well, research moves faster. If they are fragile, science slows down.

I would bring a practical, production-minded approach to that problem. I have built systems where research, product, infrastructure, and operations all had to meet in the same architecture. I am comfortable making core technical decisions, debugging distributed systems under ambiguity, building the tooling needed to see what is happening, and creating enough structure that teams can keep increasing model and product ambition without the platform becoming the bottleneck.

Iso's mission is also a serious part of the draw. I want to work on AI infrastructure where better systems can directly accelerate scientific discovery and, ultimately, medicine. The ML Ops role looks like a place where my background in AI platform engineering, secure execution, reliability, and fast-moving applied ML product work would be genuinely useful.
