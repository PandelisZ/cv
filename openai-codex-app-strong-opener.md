# OpenAI Codex App Strong Opener

Prepared for:
- Pandelis Zembashis

Purpose:
- Start the recruiter screen strongly
- Set expectations about communication style
- Give the recruiter permission to interrupt and redirect
- Signal confidence without sounding defensive or chaotic

## The goal of the opener

The opener needs to do four things quickly:

1. Establish that I am a serious fit for `Codex App`
2. Give the recruiter permission to interrupt me if I go long
3. Prevent British-style underselling
4. Frame my ADHD and conversational style as something manageable, not something awkward

The recruiter does not need my life story.
They need:
- a sharp first impression
- a clear sense of fit
- a sense of how to manage the conversation well

## The meta opener I should use at the top

This is the best version:

> Before I jump in, one quick note on communication style. I think out loud and I can sometimes answer in a very high-context way, so if I go too broad or too long, please feel free to interrupt and steer me. I genuinely find that helpful. Also, being British, I sometimes understate my work a bit, so I’m trying to be more explicit than usual about scope and impact.

Why this works:
- It is direct
- It is self-aware
- It gives the recruiter permission to manage the call
- It does not sound apologetic
- It prevents them from reading tangents as lack of clarity

## More explicit ADHD version

Use this only if you want to be very direct:

> Before I jump in, one quick note on how I communicate. I have ADHD, so I can sometimes think out loud and give more context than needed. If I go too long or drift, please cut in and redirect me. I won’t take that badly at all. It actually helps me give better answers.

This version is strong because it is plain and practical.
It does not ask for sympathy.
It gives the recruiter a clear instruction.

## Best combined version

This is probably the strongest one for you:

> Before I jump in, one quick note on communication style. I have ADHD, so I sometimes answer in a very high-context or free-flowing way. If I go too broad or too long, please feel free to cut in and redirect me. I find that helpful. This is probably the one to use.

## How to make strong claims without sounding sloppy

Avoid this:

> We were Codex before Codex.

Use this instead:

> I’ve been building in this category long enough to have a pretty clear view of where these systems succeed and where they break down in real use.

Or:

> A big part of what makes me interested in Codex App is that I’ve already been building adjacent systems, so I’m not reasoning from hype. I’m reasoning from having worked through the product and architecture tradeoffs directly.

Or:

> I think I’m a strong fit because I’ve already spent a meaningful amount of time building the same class of product, not just using it.

## How to talk about outsized impact and small-team leverage

Avoid this:

> We’re playing in another league given our size.

Use this instead:

> One thing I’m proud of is the amount of surface area we were able to cover with a relatively small team. It forced us to be sharp about architecture and reuse, because we couldn’t afford heavy or fragmented implementations.

Or:

> Working in a small team meant I had to operate with fairly outsized scope across product, architecture, and implementation. That suits me well, and I think it’s relevant to a fast-moving team like Codex.

Or:

> Because the team was lean, a lot of the work required broad ownership. I was regularly thinking across product surface, system boundaries, portability, and developer experience rather than just one isolated layer.

## How to talk about being "British" without being weird about it

Avoid:

> Don’t let me be British.

Use:

> I can sometimes default to understatement, so I’m trying to be direct about scope and impact.

That gets the point across without sounding performative.

- It is adult
- It communicates a practical adjustment
- It keeps you in control of the framing

## If you start talking too much, use one of these self-corrections

- `Let me give you the short version.`
- `The main point is...`
- `The reason that matters for this role is...`
- `I’ll stop there unless you want more detail.`
- `The short answer is yes, and the relevant part is...`

These are important because they let you recover without waiting for the recruiter to cut in.

## If you interrupt them by accident

Use:

> Sorry, go ahead.

Or:

> Sorry, I cut across you there. Please continue.

Do not over-apologize.
One sentence is enough.

## Questions to ask early to show engagement

You do not need many.
One or two well-placed questions is enough.

Good examples:

- `Between the app role and the more core/runtime-oriented role, where do you think my background maps most strongly?`
- `For the app team specifically, what are the biggest product or architecture problems you’re hoping this hire will take on?`
- `How much of the role is currently building new workflows versus hardening the foundations of the product?`

## Transparent assumptions

If you are unsure, say it cleanly.

Use:

> I may be missing some internal context, but from the outside it looks like...

Or:

> I’m making an external assumption here, so happy to be corrected, but...

This shows judgment.
It is much better than pretending certainty.

## The tone to aim for

You want:
- sharp
- self-aware
- high-agency
- technical
- product-minded

You do not want:
- apologetic
- chaotic
- overfamiliar
- too self-deprecating
- too performative

## The one-line reminder before the call

> Be direct about impact. Ask for interruption explicitly. Keep the answer anchored to Codex App, not AI in general.
