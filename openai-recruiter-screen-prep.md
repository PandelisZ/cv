## Core positioning

Strongest fit:
- `Codex Core Agents` first
- `Codex App` second

Why:
- `Core Agents` is centered on agent runtime, execution, orchestration, state, reliability, latency, cost, and sandboxing.
- `App` is centered on desktop and IDE workflows, TypeScript/Node/Electron, IPC, Rust integration, and end-to-end developer UX.
- Your Cosine story appears to map most directly to the agent kernel and production behavior layer.

Primary framing:
- You have already been building coding-agent systems before the category fully solidified.
- Your value is not just model enthusiasm. It is knowing how to turn model capability into dependable developer workflows.
- You understand the real production problems: tool execution, long-running workflows, retries, cancellation, observability, latency, trust, and product feedback loops.

## Best answers to likely recruiter questions

### Why OpenAI?

Suggested answer:

> I want to work where the model layer, infrastructure layer, and product layer can improve together. OpenAI is unusual because Codex is tightly connected to frontier model work as well as real product deployment. That creates a much stronger feedback loop between model capability, runtime behavior, and user experience. That’s where I think I can have the most leverage. I also care about building systems that are safe and dependable enough to earn real trust, not just impress in demos.

### Why this role?

For `Core Agents`:

> This role is compelling because it focuses on the actual kernel of the agent: execution environments, orchestration, memory and state, reliability, and the quality-speed-cost tradeoffs that determine whether the product works in the real world.

For `App`:

> This role is compelling because developer agents live or die on workflow quality. Clean boundaries across UI, Node and Electron layers, IPC, process orchestration, and Rust services are what make the system feel fast and dependable.

### Why now / why leave Cosine?

> I’ve learned a lot by being early in the category. What I want now is to work in an environment with tighter coupling to frontier model work and larger-scale deployment, where improvements at the runtime and product layer can compound with improvements in the model itself.

### What are you looking for next?

> A role with hard technical problems, real user impact, and high ownership. I’m especially interested in turning ambiguous agent capability into robust systems developers actually rely on.

### Which team is the better fit?

> If I had to choose one, Core Agents. That’s where my recent experience maps most directly. App is also interesting to me because I care a lot about workflow and usability, but Core Agents is probably the tighter fit.

## What to emphasize from Cosine

- You understand coding agents as a production problem, not a demo problem.
- You’ve seen real failure modes: bad tool contracts, flaky execution, weak state handling, retry and cancel issues, latency tradeoffs, and trust erosion.
- You can connect model behavior to user workflow.
- You can operate in ambiguity.

## What not to do

- Do not open with “I was building Codex before Codex” as a boast.
- Better framing: “I’ve been building coding agents in this space for a while, so I’ve seen firsthand where they fail in production.”
- Do not overshare confidential details from Cosine.
- Do not sound like you are applying to anything at OpenAI.
- Do not give a generic “I want to make impact” answer without role-specific detail.

## Questions to ask the recruiter

- Between Core Agents and App, where do you think my background is the tighter fit?
- What are the most important problems these teams need this hire to take on in the first 6 to 12 months?
- How distinct are the two teams in practice, and how much do they interface day to day?
- What tends to make candidates stand out in the process for Codex engineering roles?
- How do you think about leveling for engineers who operate across product and systems?
- What are the location expectations for these teams?
