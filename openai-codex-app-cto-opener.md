`Why is a CTO applying for a software engineer role?`

- you like building

- you are optimizing for problem quality, team quality, and scope of impact
- you are not trying to escape management or title inflation

## How to handle the "why move from CTO to software engineer?" question

This is the critical answer.

> The short answer is that I’m optimizing for the quality of the problem and the team, not for title preservation. At Cosine, I was hired in as CTO rather than as a co-founder, and I stepped into that role because it was what the company needed at that stage. I’ve loved working with the team, I believe in what we’re building, and I’ve had the chance to help shape both the product and the engineering organization in a meaningful way.
>
> At this point, the product is in a strong place, the foundations are good, and the team is in a good place as well. So for me this is not about running away from anything. It is about being deliberate about where I want to spend the next phase of my time. What attracts me to Codex App is that it is unusually aligned with the work I actually like doing most: deep product and systems building at the boundary between UX and architecture. So I do not see this as stepping away from seniority. I see it as moving toward the most technically and product-interesting version of the work.

Shorter:

> I’m not attached to title for its own sake. I care about leverage, technical depth, and working on the most important version of the problem.

More personal version:

> I was hired in as CTO rather than joining as a co-founder, and I stepped up into the role because it was what Cosine needed. I’ve genuinely loved the team and believe in the product, and I’m proud of the foundations and organization we’ve built. This is less about leaving a bad situation and more about being honest that the title itself was never the point for me. The point was building something important well, and Codex App is one of the few roles I’ve seen that feels genuinely worth moving for.

What not to say directly:

- `Cosine was never really endgame.`
- `I never planned to be CTO there long term.`

Those may be true internally, but in a recruiter screen they can sound transactional or dismissive. The stronger version is:

> I took on the CTO role because it was what the company needed, and I’m proud of what we’ve built. I’m now being deliberate about the next problem and team I want to commit to.
